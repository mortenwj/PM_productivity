Process Framework – Identity & Positioning (v1)
1. Anchor Identity
Product Anchor: Productized process platform with multiple consumption models
The Process Framework is positioned first and foremost as a product platform, not a consulting offering and not a single hosted service. It provides a standardized, configurable foundation for end-to-end public-sector processes, with different commercial and delivery models layered on top.
In short: One platform – multiple ways to consume value.
2. One-Sentence Positioning
For public-sector organizations that need compliant, efficient, end-to-end case handling, the Process Framework is a configurable process platform that standardizes and automates core workflows—without bespoke development—while supporting both self-service configuration and ready-made hosted processes.
3. What It Is / What It Is Not
What it is
A productized process platform embedded in Public 360°
A standardized yet configurable foundation for end-to-end processes
A platform that includes a curated collection of process model templates and process engine templates to ensure fast adoption
A starting point, not a blank canvas: customers get ready-made structures they can configure and extend
A scalable alternative to custom process development
A value multiplier for other Public 360° capabilities (e-services, documents, integrations, AI)
A platform designed for reuse, consistency, and continuous improvement across customers
What it is not
❌ A one-off consulting framework
❌ A fully bespoke process development service
❌ Just a template library (templates are included, but value comes from the platform + execution + rules)
❌ A static collection of diagrams or documentation
❌ A single-purpose workflow engine
4. Mental Model (How Customers Should Think About It)
“A process operating system for Public 360°.”
The framework defines how work flows, what must happen, and when decisions are allowed
Customers plug in:
Standard processes
Rules and compliance logic
Documents, integrations, and automations
They choose how much they want to configure themselves vs. consume as a managed service
5. Platform Layers & Value Acceleration
The Process Framework is structured in clear layers to separate core product value from adoption acceleration and optional extensions. This ensures fast customer adoption while preserving long-term scalability and pricing clarity.
5.1 Core Platform (Always Included)
The core platform is always included as part of the Process Framework subscription and represents the non-negotiable foundation of the product.
It consists of three tightly integrated components:
Process Models – define structure, phases, milestones, and transitions
Process Engine – executes workflows, automation, and integrations
Business Rules Engine (BRE) – governs decisions, compliance, and constraints
These components together form the execution heart of the framework and enable standardized, automated, and compliant end-to-end processes.
5.2 Adoption Accelerators (Included by Default)
To ensure rapid time-to-value and reduce implementation risk, the platform includes a curated set of adoption accelerators by default.
Included accelerators:
Process model templates for common public-sector case types
Process engine templates with preconfigured automation and flow logic
Standard process library built on validated customer and pilot use cases
Best-practice configurations for phases, milestones, and checklists
These accelerators ensure customers do not start from a blank canvas, while still allowing full configuration and extension within the platform.
5.3 Extensions & Add-ons (Optional)
Extensions are optional capabilities and services that allow customers to scale, specialize, or accelerate beyond the default platform.
Examples include:
Custom or domain-specific process development
Advanced automation and integration scenarios
Additional process packs or specialized templates
Advisory services, workshops, and training
This layer supports differentiated customer needs without fragmenting the core product.
6. Commercial Consumption & Pricing Models
The Process Framework is delivered as one unified platform. All customers access the same core capabilities and adoption accelerators. Differentiation is achieved through how the platform is consumed and paid for, not through feature fragmentation.
This ensures:
A single product roadmap
Predictable product behavior across customers
Flexible commercial models aligned to customer maturity
6.1 Consumption Model 1: Single Process Access
Purpose: Low-barrier entry and targeted use cases.
Customers pay for access to one specific end-to-end process, delivered on top of the unified platform.
Characteristics:
Access limited to one activated process
Platform capabilities are used but not fully exposed
Ideal for small organizations or isolated needs
Acts as an entry point to broader platform adoption
Commercial intent:
Transactional or limited subscription pricing
Clear upgrade path to broader access models
6.2 Consumption Model 2: End-to-End Process Subscription
Purpose: Standardized, repeatable value with fast time-to-market.
Customers subscribe to a set of standardized end-to-end processes (Hosted Processes), fully managed and continuously improved.
Characteristics:
Predefined processes with limited configuration
Platform is consumed as a managed service
Low operational overhead for the customer
Predictable pricing and rapid onboarding
Commercial intent:
Subscription priced per process or process bundle
Optimized for volume and scalability
6.3 Consumption Model 3: Full Platform Access
Purpose: Maximum flexibility and long-term value creation.
Customers subscribe to full access to the Process Framework, including self-service configuration and modeling.
Characteristics:
Full access to platform capabilities
Ability to configure, extend, and govern processes independently
Supports complex, cross-domain process landscapes
Designed for mature or large organizations
Commercial intent:
Subscription pricing anchored in organizational value and scale
Optional add-ons for services and advanced capabilities
6.4 Strategic Principle
One platform. Multiple pricing models. Clear upgrade paths.
Customers can start small and expand their usage over time without migrating products or changing architecture.
7. Pricing Dimensions (Architecture)
Pricing for the Process Framework is anchored in organizational tiering, with controlled variation based on process scope and complexity. This makes pricing predictable, procurement-friendly, and easy to scale.
7.1 Primary Pricing Anchor: Organization Tier
The organization tier is the primary unit for quoting and contracting.
Typical tier indicators:
Organization size (e.g. small, medium, large municipality or agency)
Estimated annual case volume
Number of users / departments involved
Each tier defines:
A base platform subscription
A set of included process entitlements
Expected support and governance level
Rationale:
Aligns with how public-sector organizations budget and procure
Provides predictable and transparent pricing
Creates a stable foundation for long-term subscriptions
7.2 Included Process Entitlements (per Tier)
Each organization tier includes a defined level of process usage.
Examples of entitlements:
Number of active end-to-end processes included
Allowed complexity level (e.g. automation, integrations, rules)
Access to standard process and engine templates
This ensures customers clearly understand what is included before additional costs apply.
7.3 Variable Expansion: Process Scope & Complexity
Beyond the included entitlements, pricing scales with process scope and complexity.
Expansion triggers may include:
Activating additional end-to-end processes beyond the included amount
Introducing higher automation or integration complexity
Covering additional domains or case types
Commercial handling:
Step-up pricing or add-on subscriptions
Clear, predefined price increments
7.4 Guardrails for Sales and Customers
To avoid ad-hoc pricing and negotiation drift, the model enforces clear guardrails:
Organization tier sets the starting price
Process entitlements are explicit and measurable
All expansions follow predefined pricing rules
This ensures consistency across deals while remaining flexible for different customer needs.
7.2 Dimension B: Process Scope & Complexity
Pricing also scales with how broadly and deeply the platform is used.
Typical scope indicators:
Number of active end-to-end processes
Complexity of processes (phases, milestones, rules)
Degree of automation and integration
Number of domains or case types covered
Rationale:
More processes and higher complexity unlock more efficiency and automation
Encourages expansion while keeping entry barriers low
7.3 Combined Pricing Logic
Customers pay more as they grow larger and as they activate more processes and complexity.
This combined model:
Avoids penalizing small customers
Rewards gradual adoption and expansion
Aligns price with realized value over time

11. Core Elements of the Process Platform
At its core, the Process Framework is a process platform built around three tightly integrated components. Together, these components form the heart of the framework and enable standardized, automated, and compliant end-to-end processes.
11.1 Process Models
Purpose: Define what the process looks like.
Process Models describe the structural blueprint of a case or workflow. They define how work is organized from start to finish and provide a shared, standardized understanding of the process.
Key characteristics:
Definition of phases, states, milestones, and transitions
Reusable and versioned process templates
Adaptable to different case types and domains
Supports standardization while allowing local configuration
Process Models ensure consistency, predictability, and transparency across cases and organizations.
11.2 Process Engine
Purpose: Execute how the process runs.
The Process Engine is the execution layer that brings process models to life. It orchestrates tasks, automation, integrations, and user interactions across the lifecycle of a case.
Key characteristics:
Event-driven execution of process steps
Automation of tasks, handoffs, and system actions
Integration with e-services, documents, notifications, and external systems
Supports both synchronous and asynchronous processing
The Process Engine ensures reliable flow, reduces manual work, and enables true end-to-end automation.
11.3 Business Rules Engine (BRE)
Purpose: Decide when actions are allowed or required.
The Business Rules Engine evaluates conditions and enforces rules independently of hard-coded logic. It ensures compliance, quality, and consistent decision-making across all processes.
Key characteristics:
No-code / low-code rule definition
Event-, state-, and time-based rule triggers
Separation of rules from process logic
Explainability and traceability of decisions
The BRE enables rapid adaptation to regulatory changes and reduces risk by embedding compliance directly into the process platform.
11.4 How the Components Work Together
Process Models define the structure
Process Engine executes the flow
Business Rules Engine governs decisions and constraints
Together, they form a cohesive platform that supports automation, governance, and continuous improvement—without bespoke development.
